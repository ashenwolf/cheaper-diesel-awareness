How we counted

The calculator chains five steps. Each step is simple; the uncertainty is in the inputs, so every derived number is shown as a range. The low end uses the cautious bound of every uncertain input, the high end the other bound. Where we had to assume, the table below says so.

1. Pump saving to crude price

We assume taxes, refining and retail margins stay fixed, so the whole saving has to come from cheaper crude. In reality margins absorb part of any crude drop, so this understates how much oil is needed.

crude drop ($/bbl) = saving / (1 + VAT) x USD per local currency x units per barrel

2. Crude price to extra supply

extra supply = world supply x (crude drop / Brent) / price flexibility

Price flexibility is the % change in price for a 1% change in supply. The result is capped at what Russia could realistically add. Past the cap, the receipt says the rest of the discount needs someone else's oil.

3. Extra supply to Russian state revenue

revenue = state take x 30 x [(exports + extra) x (Brent - drop - discount') - exports x (Brent - discount)]

Cheaper oil also means Russia earns less on barrels it already sells, and the formula nets that out. Sanctions relief narrows the Urals discount; we move it from the current level toward the 2025 average in proportion to how much extra Russian oil is let in. Only the state's share counts, not the full sale price.

4. Revenue to drones

drones = revenue / unit cost of one Geran-2 (Shahed-136)

Russia's drone output is limited by factories, not only by cash, and money is fungible across its war budget. So the receipt says the money is enough to pay for these drones, not that Russia will buy exactly these drones.

5. Drones to civilian casualties

casualties = drones x average monthly long-range casualties x drone share / average monthly drones launched

The UN monitoring mission reports missiles and long-range drones as one category and does not attribute casualties to individual weapons. We split that category with an assumed drone share and divide by drones launched, decoys included, which lowers the ratio.

The rate is averaged over recent months rather than one, because casualties rose sharply in summer 2026. A month joins the average only when it has both a long-range casualty figure and a drone count; months still missing one are named on the receipt and in the table below. Child figures use the child share of all casualties over the months where the UN published child counts.

Display rule: when the upper estimate would exceed the toll drones actually caused that month, the receipt stops at the real toll and marks it with a plus sign. A linear forecast above observed reality is not evidence.

Known weaknesses

  • The result is most sensitive to price flexibility and state take, the two least certain inputs.
  • Russian exports count seaborne crude and products only. Pipeline barrels would also lose value when world prices fall, so including them would lower the revenue estimate. This is the main way the model could overstate.
  • Casualty data lags by about two weeks, so the reference month is not the current month.
  • The Ukrainian Air Force reports drones of all types together, including decoys.
  • Prices are averages. Your local pump will differ.
